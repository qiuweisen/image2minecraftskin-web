**Quick answer:** ChartMini and CandleDojo solve different practice jobs. **ChartMini is better suited to open-ended historical replay where you decide whether to buy, sell, hold, change quantity, or do nothing while later candles remain hidden. CandleDojo is better suited to short, scored chart-reading drills where you must commit Long or Short before a fixed replay outcome is revealed.**

Neither tool is a broker, and neither should be treated as proof that a strategy will make money live.

If your main question is **“Can I read this chart direction correctly?”**, CandleDojo gives the more structured feedback loop. If your question is **“Can I follow a broader decision process through an unfolding historical chart?”**, ChartMini gives you more control over the replay sequence and simulated position decisions.

## The Main Difference Is the Training Loop

The most useful way to compare these tools is not to ask which one has more features. Ask what behavior each tool forces you to practice.

### CandleDojo: read, commit, score

CandleDojo describes itself as a chart-reading gym. Its current workflow is deliberately narrow:

1. view a frozen historical chart;
2. use higher- and lower-timeframe context;
3. commit **Long** or **Short**;
4. replay the next candles;
5. receive a scored result and feedback.

Its public [methodology](https://candledojo.app/methodology) says the scored answer is based on the direction rewarded by a fixed 30-candle replay window. Scenarios are accepted only when that replay-window winner also aligns with its higher-timeframe EMA framework. CandleDojo also states that flat or ambiguous periods are filtered from the scenario library.

That structure is useful when the skill you want to isolate is **pre-outcome chart reading under a consistent scoring rule**.

It is not the same thing as paper trading or a broker simulator, and CandleDojo is explicit about that on its [About page](https://candledojo.app/about): it does not provide a virtual balance, position sizing, live execution, or a full backtesting workflow.

### ChartMini: replay, decide, manage the simulated sequence

ChartMini starts from a different exercise. Instead of giving you a predefined directional question, it loads historical market data and lets the session unfold while future candles remain hidden.

In the core replay workflow you can:

- buy;
- sell or short;
- change simulated quantity;
- hold an existing simulated position;
- take no action;
- advance the historical chart;
- finish and review the recorded session.

Daily Replay covers stocks, forex, and crypto. Intraday Replay currently uses historical five-minute source data for forex and crypto, with broader chart periods available for viewing the same replay path. The [ChartMini market replay tutorial](/blog/market-replay-how-to-practice-trading-with-historical-charts) documents the current workflow and its limitations.

ChartMini therefore gives you a less scripted question: not simply “up or down?”, but **“what, if anything, does my process tell me to do before I see the next bar?”**

That flexibility is useful, but it also means the user has to bring more structure to the session.

## Where CandleDojo Is Stronger

A fair comparison should identify the situations where the competitor is the better fit.

### 1. Immediate per-rep scoring

CandleDojo has the clearer answer if you want every chart-reading rep to end with an explicit correct/incorrect result.

Its scoring system uses one documented framework, and its methodology explains what “correct” means. That makes results easy to track over repeated reps.

ChartMini records simulated trading outcomes, but it does **not** assign a standardized directional grade to every chart state. A profitable simulated action and a high-quality decision are not necessarily the same thing, so ChartMini requires more deliberate review.

### 2. Forced commitment before the outcome

CandleDojo requires a Long or Short call before the replay. That prevents a user from passively watching the chart and later convincing themselves that they “would have known” what to do.

ChartMini also hides future bars, but it allows a no-trade decision and a wider range of simulated actions. That is more flexible, but less constrained.

### 3. Two-timeframe drill structure

CandleDojo currently presents a higher-timeframe context chart and a lower-timeframe entry chart in each scenario. Its public methodology lists three pairs:

- 15-minute / 1-minute;
- 4-hour / 15-minute;
- Daily / 4-hour.

For someone specifically training top-down recognition, having that structure prebuilt can reduce setup work.

### 4. Built-in progression and motivation

CandleDojo tracks XP, belts, streaks, accuracy, and pattern-level progress. Its belt system is based on cumulative XP rather than being presented as proof of live trading performance.

For users who stay consistent because practice feels like a short daily drill, that is a real product advantage.

## Where ChartMini Is Stronger

ChartMini's advantage is not that it “proves” a user can trade. It does not. Its advantage is that the exercise can extend beyond a single directional call.

### 1. Open-ended no-trade decisions

A real trading process often includes waiting.

In ChartMini, you can look at the visible history and decide that no setup exists. You can advance the chart without creating a position and see whether your original read still holds.

That makes it easier to practice questions such as:

- Did I enter because my setup appeared, or because I was bored?
- Did I chase after a move was already extended?
- Did I keep the same rule when the chart became messy?
- Could I stay out when conditions did not qualify?

CandleDojo intentionally removes that ambiguity by asking for a Long/Short decision inside a scenario selected for its framework. That is useful for recognition training, but it is a different task.

### 2. Simulated positions and quantity changes

ChartMini maintains a simplified virtual position and P&L record. You can buy, sell, reduce, close, or reverse simulated exposure and change quantity during the replay.

This does **not** make ChartMini a broker emulator. The current product does not model every spread, fee, partial fill, queue position, latency effect, margin rule, borrow condition, or intrabar sequence.

It also does not provide native stop-loss or take-profit order placement. If your exercise depends on a stop, target, or invalidation level, record that rule separately and judge whether you followed it as the replay unfolds. The [no-signup simulator guide](/blog/free-trading-simulator-no-signup) documents these current product boundaries.

### 3. Broader daily market coverage

ChartMini's Daily Replay currently covers three market categories:

- stocks;
- forex;
- crypto.

Its intraday environment currently focuses on forex and crypto.

CandleDojo's current official [data page](https://candledojo.app/data) describes a smaller, deeper practice set centered on BTC/USDT, EUR/USD, USD/JPY, and XAU/USD. That focus is reasonable for a curated training library, but it is less broad than ChartMini's daily market categories.

### 4. No account required for the core replay loop

ChartMini's core replay opens in the browser without requiring an email address, broker account, card, or software installation.

CandleDojo has also reduced signup friction since earlier versions of this comparison. Its current [free challenge](https://candledojo.app/challenge) allows guest rounds without an account, while creating a free account is used to save progress and access the larger daily rep allowance.

So the old claim that CandleDojo “requires an account to start” is no longer accurate.

## Feedback: The Two Tools Measure Different Things

This is the most important point in the comparison.

CandleDojo's feedback answers a narrow question:

> Did your Long/Short read match the side rewarded by this scenario's fixed replay window and methodology?

That is clean, repeatable feedback for a chart-reading drill.

ChartMini's feedback answers a different set of questions:

- What simulated actions did you take?
- What quantity did you use?
- What happened to the virtual position as bars unfolded?
- What was the resulting simulated P&L?
- What did the trade/session record show?

ChartMini also offers an optional analysis flow after authentication, but the core replay should still be reviewed as a record of decisions rather than as a readiness score.

Neither feedback system should be interpreted as evidence that a user has a profitable live-trading edge.

A CandleDojo accuracy score measures performance inside CandleDojo's scoring framework. A ChartMini simulated P&L result measures the outcome of simplified historical decisions under ChartMini's replay assumptions. **Those are practice metrics, not live-performance guarantees.**

## Curated Scenarios vs Random Replay

The original version of this article treated curated scenarios as inherently inferior. That was too simplistic.

### Curated scenarios can be useful

Curation can make deliberate practice more efficient when the objective is a specific skill. CandleDojo publicly explains that it filters scenarios for directional clarity and framework consistency. This means a user can run many comparable reps without first searching for an example.

The trade-off is selection. Because ambiguous periods are excluded, the exercise does not directly test whether you would recognize that the best decision in an unfiltered market is to do nothing.

### Random replay can be useful

ChartMini's random historical sessions expose more of the ordinary sequence between obvious setups. That can help with patience, setup selection, and no-trade decisions.

The trade-off is structure. Random replay does not automatically tell you what skill to practice or whether a particular decision followed a valid rule. Without a written objective and review process, a replay session can become aimless clicking.

For that reason, use the [trading simulator evaluation checklist](/blog/evaluate-trading-simulator-features) before deciding which practice format fits the job.

## Which Tool Is Better for Beginners?

There is no universal winner because “beginner” covers several different needs.

### Choose ChartMini first if you want to learn the replay process

ChartMini is the simpler starting point when you want to:

- open a historical chart immediately;
- practice without creating an account;
- see future bars revealed gradually;
- record basic buy/sell/hold/no-trade decisions;
- experiment with simulated quantity and position management;
- practice across stock, forex, and crypto daily charts.

A complete beginner still needs basic chart-reading education. The simulator does not replace learning what OHLC candles, trend, support/resistance, or risk rules mean.

### Choose CandleDojo first if you already know the basics and want recognition reps

CandleDojo's own positioning says it is strongest for traders who already understand candlesticks, support/resistance, and basic price action but want faster, more consistent chart reads.

That makes sense. The fixed Long/Short decision, immediate result, pattern tracking, and repeatable scoring reduce the amount of self-review required after each rep.

## Can You Use ChartMini and CandleDojo Together?

Yes. The products are more complementary than the old version of this article suggested.

A practical two-stage routine could be:

1. **CandleDojo for focused recognition reps** — use several short scenarios to test directional reading under a consistent framework.
2. **ChartMini for open-ended replay** — move to an unfiltered historical sequence and require yourself to identify when a setup exists, when it does not, and how your simulated decisions evolve over multiple bars.
3. **Journal the difference** — record whether a pattern you recognized in a curated drill remained useful when the surrounding chart was less structured.
4. **Do not convert either score into a live-capital rule** — use both tools as practice evidence, not as a license to increase real risk.

For a broader explanation of where replay, paper trading, and strategy testing fit, see [Market Replay vs Backtesting vs Paper Trading](/blog/market-replay-vs-backtesting-vs-paper-trading).

## What Neither Tool Simulates Well

Both products are educational practice environments, not substitutes for live execution evidence.

Depending on the market and exercise, neither should be assumed to reproduce all of the following:

- live bid/ask spread behavior;
- queue position and partial fills;
- latency and order-routing differences;
- changing commissions, fees, financing, and borrow conditions;
- full order-book or Level 2 behavior;
- real account restrictions and broker margin rules;
- the emotional effect of actual financial loss;
- future market behavior.

CandleDojo is especially explicit that it is not a paper-trading or backtesting platform. ChartMini likewise documents that its candle-based replay uses simplified hypothetical fills and is not a broker simulator.

If order-entry realism is the main goal, a broker's own demo or paper environment may be a better fit than either product.

## Final Verdict

**CandleDojo is the stronger choice for a narrow, scored chart-reading workout. ChartMini is the stronger choice for less scripted historical replay where you want to make and review a broader sequence of simulated trading decisions.**

Choose by the skill you are trying to train:

| Your practice goal | Better fit |
|---|---|
| Make fast Long/Short chart reads and get an immediate grade | **CandleDojo** |
| Track accuracy, XP, streaks, and pattern-level progress | **CandleDojo** |
| Practice with paired higher/lower timeframe scenarios already prepared | **CandleDojo** |
| Replay random historical sequences without an account | **ChartMini** |
| Practice buy/sell/hold/no-trade decisions across an unfolding chart | **ChartMini** |
| Use simplified simulated positions, quantity, and P&L | **ChartMini** |
| Practice daily charts across stocks, forex, and crypto | **ChartMini** |
| Learn a broker order ticket, live fills, or detailed execution mechanics | **Neither — use an appropriate broker demo/paper environment** |

The important distinction is not “quiz versus real trading.” Both are simulations with limitations. The useful distinction is **structured recognition drill versus open-ended replay practice**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is CandleDojo free?

CandleDojo's current public pages describe free guest practice and a free account tier, with no credit card required for the challenge. Its guest and account limits can change, so verify the current [CandleDojo challenge page](https://candledojo.app/challenge) before relying on a specific daily allowance.

### Does CandleDojo require an account?

Not to try the current guest challenge. An account is used to save progress and unlock the larger free daily practice allowance and progress features.

### Does ChartMini have scoring like CandleDojo?

Not the same kind. CandleDojo gives a standardized correct/incorrect directional result for each scenario. ChartMini records simplified simulated positions, P&L, and session actions instead of grading every chart state with one directional answer.

### Can ChartMini place stop-loss or take-profit orders?

No. ChartMini's current core replay uses simplified Buy, Sell, quantity, and replay controls. A stop, target, or invalidation rule should be recorded separately; ChartMini should not be described as a full broker-order simulator.

### Which tool is better for candlestick-pattern practice?

CandleDojo is more structured for repeated pattern-recognition and directional drills because it provides curated scenarios, two-timeframe context, scoring, and post-round feedback. ChartMini is better when you want to find and interpret patterns inside a longer, less scripted replay sequence.

### Which tool is better for practicing position management?

ChartMini is closer to that task because it maintains simplified simulated positions, quantity, and P&L across the replay. It still does not model all broker order types or live execution conditions, so use a broker demo when those details matter.
