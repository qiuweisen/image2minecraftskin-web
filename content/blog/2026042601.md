### CFTC: Eight Things You Should Know Before Trading Forex

The CFTC's [Eight Things You Should Know Before Trading Forex](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/CustomerAdvisory_MustKnowForex.html) is one of the most useful first references for a person considering United States retail OTC forex.

It explains issues that generic beginner ebooks often omit:

- the dealer is the counterparty in an OTC transaction;
- the dealer controls the platform and displayed information;
- closing or offsetting a position depends on the dealer's conditions;
- deposits may not receive the protections a reader assumes;
- margin can amplify losses;
- salespeople, influencers, and affiliates may have conflicts;
- registration and disciplinary history should be checked before a deposit.

Use the advisory to create a **dealer-risk checklist**. Do not use it as a strategy guide or assume its United States rules apply unchanged in another jurisdiction.

The CFTC's learning-resources page provides an official PDF option for this advisory. Start from the CFTC page rather than a third-party mirror so you can confirm the publisher and current context.

### Investor.gov: Forex Trading for Individual Investors

The SEC's Investor.gov bulletin, [Foreign Currency Exchange (Forex) Trading for Individual Investors](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/foreign), provides a concise reference for:

- non-uniform quote conventions;
- transaction costs;
- leverage and margin;
- automated-system risk;
- potential fraud;
- the need to understand the dealer agreement.

The publication is older than many commercial ebooks, but age alone does not make a risk principle useless. It does mean that any leverage limit, account protection, legal reference, platform process, or provider term must be checked against current official sources.

A useful way to save this material is to print or export the official webpage for personal reference while retaining the original URL and access date. A saved copy should not replace the live source when checking current rules.

### CME Group: A Trader's Guide to Futures

CME Group's [A Trader's Guide to Futures](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/brochures-and-handbooks/a-traders-guide-to-futures) is relevant when the product is an exchange-traded currency future.

It covers areas such as:

- what futures contracts are;
- who uses them;
- how a futures trade works;
- contract-based market structure;
- differences between futures and other products.

The main value is precision about the **futures product**. The main danger is category confusion. A currency future has a contract specification, expiry or settlement framework, exchange rules, and clearing structure that should not be copied into an OTC spot or CFD explanation.

Before using any example, write the product name at the top of your notes:

- retail OTC forex;
- rolling spot or CFD;
- exchange-traded currency future;
- deliverable currency conversion;
- another derivative.

If the document and your intended account product do not match, treat the document as comparative background rather than operating instructions.

### CFTC Forex Fraud Resources

The [CFTC Forex Frauds hub](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/forexfrauds) gathers advisories about fake trading sites, social-media solicitations, paid promoters, withdrawal demands, crypto-only deposits, and unregistered entities.

This belongs in a beginner reference pack because many free PDFs are not neutral education. They may be the first step in a funnel leading to:

- an unregistered dealer;
- a messaging group;
- paid signals;
- account management;
- a copy-trading offer;
- a training upsell;
- a request for identity or payment information.

A free document is not automatically fraudulent, and a commercial relationship does not automatically make information false. The correct response is to identify the relationship, verify the claims, and refuse to treat testimonials or screenshots as independent evidence.

For a United States-facing firm or salesperson, use the official registration and disciplinary-history tools referenced by the CFTC, including [NFA BASIC](https://www.nfa.futures.org/basicnet/). Other jurisdictions have different registers and permissions.

## What a Useful Beginner PDF Should Contain

Do not require every document to cover the entire subject. A focused risk advisory can be useful without teaching chart patterns. A product manual can be useful without teaching macroeconomics. Evaluate whether the document fulfils its stated purpose.

For a broad beginner guide, look for clear coverage or explicit links to the following areas.

### Product and Counterparty

The document should identify what is being traded and who stands on the other side or processes the transaction.

Questions to answer:

- Is this OTC forex, a CFD, rolling spot, a currency future, or physical conversion?
- Is the provider a dealer, broker, exchange member, or another type of entity?
- Which legal entity and jurisdiction govern the account?
- Is delivery expected, or is the position normally offset or rolled?

Use the [retail forex mechanics guide](/blog/forex-trading-beginner-breakdown) when the PDF uses terms such as spot, lot, pip, margin, and rollover without explaining the transaction lifecycle.

### Quotes and Execution

A useful document distinguishes:

- base and quote currency;
- bid and ask;
- displayed chart price and executable price;
- order instruction and actual fill;
- trigger price and fill price;
- gross price movement and net account result.

A screenshot is only useful when it identifies the platform, product, time, price side, and purpose. An attractive chart without those labels is not sufficient operating evidence.

The [Forex chart-reading guide](/blog/how-to-read-forex-chart-candlesticks-explained) owns chart source, axes, timeframe, OHLC, live-bar, and provider-difference checks.

### Leverage, Margin, and Loss

A PDF should not present leverage only as the ability to control a large position with a small deposit.

It should separate:

- notional exposure;
- initial or required margin;
- maintenance or close-out rules where applicable;
- equity and available margin;
- planned loss;
- gap and execution risk;
- the possibility that account rules differ by entity and product.

Reject fixed universal leverage or deposit recommendations. A historical example can explain arithmetic, but it is not a current account rule.

### Costs and Holding Conditions

Look for:

- bid-ask spread;
- commission;
- financing or rollover;
- conversion charges;
- data or platform charges where applicable;
- slippage and execution conditions;
- withdrawal and inactivity terms where applicable.

A PDF with a fixed spread table can become stale quickly. Use the document to learn the cost categories, then verify current figures on the exact provider's official pages and account documents. The [rates, rollover, and spread verification guide](/blog/combining-central-bank-rates-and-spread-for-better-results) owns that evidence workflow.

### Risk and Conflicts

The resource should not hide risk below a long performance story.

Warning signs include:

- guaranteed or unusually consistent returns;
- a fixed win rate presented without complete methodology and independent evidence;
- claims that a beginner can replace employment quickly;
- pressure to deposit before understanding the product;
- screenshots with no account, time-period, fee, or verification context;
- an affiliate or referral link with no clear disclosure;
- a free file that immediately moves the reader into private messaging.

The FTC notes that investment scams often begin with free training, materials, or videos and then escalate into expensive coaching or supposed proven systems. The FTC also expects material relationships behind endorsements to be disclosed clearly.

### Publication and Revision Evidence

A useful file should provide enough information to identify its version:

- document title;
- publisher;
- author or responsible organization;
- publication date;
- revision date or version where available;
- source URL;
- jurisdiction;
- product scope;
- copyright or reuse terms.

An undated PDF is not automatically wrong, but its claims should be treated as unverified until checked against current official sources.

## Download Safety and Copyright Checks

The word “PDF” in a filename or search result is not evidence that the file is legitimate.

Before downloading:

1. Open the publisher's official domain directly.
2. Confirm that the download link belongs to the same organization or a clearly identified document host.
3. Check the file type. A supposed ebook should not require an executable installer, browser extension, or unknown mobile application.
4. Avoid password-protected archives from unknown sources.
5. Keep the operating system, browser, and security tools current.
6. Scan unexpected files before opening them.
7. Do not enter a broker password, wallet seed phrase, remote-access code, or payment information to unlock a free guide.
8. Check whether saving, printing, or redistribution is permitted.

A personal reference copy and a redistributed copy are not the same activity. Do not upload a paid book, private course, or online curriculum as a “free PDF” merely because another site has already copied it.

## How to Detect an Outdated Forex PDF

Use this audit rather than judging by design quality.

| Check | What to compare |
|---|---|
| Date | Publication, revision, and access date |
| Product | OTC, CFD, rolling spot, futures, or physical conversion |
| Jurisdiction | Named regulator and current legal entity |
| Leverage and margin | Current provider and regulator documents |
| Costs | Current spread, commission, financing, conversion, and withdrawal terms |
| Platform | Current screenshots, order names, and account fields |
| Market data | Source, period, instrument, and whether the number is still current |
| Links | Whether official references still resolve |
| Commercial relationship | Broker ownership, affiliate links, sponsorship, or lead capture |
| Claims | Whether returns, win rates, or testimonials have substantiation and representative context |

Do not “correct” an old historical document by silently substituting current figures. Preserve the document as historical evidence and make a separate current verification note.

## Build a Small Verified Forex Reference Pack

A beginner does not need a folder containing hundreds of ebooks. A smaller evidence pack is easier to maintain.

Suggested categories:

### 1. Product Risk

Keep the current regulator advisory relevant to your product and jurisdiction.

Record:

- exact product;
- legal entity;
- regulator or register;
- account protections and exclusions;
- counterparty structure;
- margin and close-out language;
- complaint and dispute process.

### 2. Product Specification

Keep the current provider or exchange specification for:

- contract or quantity unit;
- quote convention;
- minimum increment;
- trading or access hours;
- order types;
- financing or expiry;
- fees;
- settlement or close-out process.

### 3. Platform Instructions

Use the exact platform and account version. Record where the provider documents:

- bid and ask;
- order status;
- quantity;
- attached exits;
- margin fields;
- account history;
- statement export.

### 4. Current Information Sources

Do not rely on a static PDF for changing information such as:

- policy rates;
- economic releases;
- event calendars;
- current margin schedules;
- spreads;
- market holidays;
- daylight-saving conversions;
- platform outages;
- product availability.

Store the official live-source URLs instead.

### 5. Personal Study Records

Create your own documents rather than downloading a stranger's trading rules:

- glossary of terms you have verified;
- product-comparison sheet;
- cost and margin worksheet;
- chart-reading record;
- practice-order record;
- error log;
- source and revision log.

These are evidence records, not proof that a strategy is profitable or that you are ready to trade live.

## A Seven-Step PDF Verification Workflow

### Step 1: Define the Question

Write what you need from the resource.

Examples:

- understand OTC dealer risk;
- learn futures contract structure;
- identify bid and ask;
- understand margin terminology;
- check a provider's order types;
- find a structured beginner curriculum.

A PDF that does not answer the defined question is not useful merely because it is long.

### Step 2: Open the Original Publisher Page

Do not begin from a file mirror. Confirm the organization, domain, document title, and current access path.

### Step 3: Classify the Product and Jurisdiction

Write both at the top of your notes. Stop if the document never identifies them and the distinction materially affects the advice.

### Step 4: Record Version Evidence

Capture the publication date, revision date, access date, and source URL. Mark undated sections.

### Step 5: Separate Stable Concepts From Changing Terms

Stable concepts may include base/quote orientation or the fact that leverage amplifies exposure. Changing terms include limits, costs, account protections, schedules, product availability, and platform fields.

### Step 6: Check Commercial and Copyright Boundaries

Identify whether the publisher sells a broker account, signals, coaching, software, or a premium course. Look for affiliate and sponsorship disclosures. Confirm whether the file can be saved, printed, or shared.

### Step 7: Test Only the Appropriate Layer

Use reading to test comprehension. Use ChartMini for blind historical chart decisions. Use a provider's demo for its order ticket, bid/ask, margin fields, and account behaviour. Neither environment proves live profitability or exact production execution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a free forex trading for beginners PDF?

Yes. Regulators and market operators publish free forex or derivatives risk guides, and some education providers publish downloadable material. No single PDF covers every retail forex product, jurisdiction, platform, cost, and current market condition, so verify the publisher, date, product scope, and limitations before relying on it.

### Can I download the School of Pipsology as a PDF?

BabyPips states that its current School of Pipsology is an online course and that it does not provide the current school in printable or electronic PDF format. Use the official online course rather than an unofficial copied or repackaged PDF.

### Which forex PDF should a beginner read first?

Start with a regulator's risk guide for the exact product and jurisdiction you are considering. Then use a product-specific guide for OTC forex, CFDs, rolling spot, or exchange-traded currency futures. General strategy material should come after product, counterparty, leverage, cost, and withdrawal risks are understood.

### Are free forex PDFs from brokers reliable?

They can be useful for that provider's platform, order types, margin terminology, and account procedures, but they are commercial materials. Confirm the legal entity, jurisdiction, publication date, product specification, fees, financing, conflicts, and risk disclosure against the provider's current official documents.

### How can I tell whether a forex PDF is outdated?

Check the publication or revision date, named regulator, product terminology, leverage and margin rules, platform screenshots, links, fee examples, and whether current official pages still support the claims. Treat undated documents and fixed market statistics as historical references until reverified.

### Can ChartMini replace a forex PDF or broker demo?

No. ChartMini can support candle-by-candle chart-reading and decision practice on randomly selected historical forex data. It does not reproduce a broker's bid and ask feed, exact pair and date selection, account entity, order routing, margin, financing, slippage, settlement, withdrawals, or current economic releases.
