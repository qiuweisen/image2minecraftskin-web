When it comes to analyzing investments, there are two major schools of thought that have dominated Wall Street for decades: **Technical Analysis** and **Fundamental Analysis**.

Each approach has its devoted followers—and its critics. Understanding both will make you a more complete trader.

This page is a broad, stock-oriented comparison. Forex requires a different fundamental dataset because every currency pair compares two economies and two expectation paths. ## What Is Fundamental Analysis?

Fundamental analysis is about determining the **intrinsic value** of an asset by examining all the factors that affect its worth.

For stocks, this includes:
- **Financial statements:** Revenue, earnings, profit margins, debt levels
- **Industry analysis:** Market size, competition, growth potential
- **Economic factors:** Interest rates, inflation, GDP growth
- **Management quality:** Track record, strategy, capital allocation

The goal is to figure out what a company is truly worth, then buy when the market price is below that value (undervalued) and sell when it's above (overvalued).

**Famous fundamental analysts:** Warren Buffett, Benjamin Graham, Peter Lynch

## What Is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis ignores the company's fundamentals entirely. Instead, it focuses on **price action and volume**—the actual trading behavior shown on charts.

Technical analysts believe that:
- All known information is already reflected in the price
- Price moves in trends that tend to persist
- History tends to repeat itself due to market psychology

They use tools like:
- **Chart patterns:** Head and shoulders, triangles, flags
- **Candlestick patterns:** Hammers, engulfing patterns, dojis
- **Indicators:** Moving averages, RSI, and carefully chosen [MACD 12-26-9 settings](/blog/macd-indicator-explained-best-settings-for-trend-trading-2026)
- **Support and resistance levels**

**Famous technical analysts:** William D. Gann, John Murphy, Martin Pring

## Key Differences

| Aspect | Fundamental Analysis | Technical Analysis |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Focus | Company value | Price patterns |
| Time Horizon | Long-term | Short to medium-term |
| Data Used | Financial statements, economic data | Charts, volume, indicators |
| Goal | Find undervalued assets | Time entries and exits |
| Question Asked | "What to buy?" | "When to buy?" |

## Which One Is Better?

Here's the truth: **Neither is universally better. They serve different purposes.**

Fundamental analysis helps you find **what** to invest in. Technical analysis helps you determine **when** to buy or sell.

Many successful traders combine both approaches:
1. Use fundamentals to identify quality assets
2. Use technicals to time their entries and exits

For example, you might use fundamental analysis to determine that Apple is a great company worth owning. Then use technical analysis to wait for a pullback to a key support level before buying.

## Which Should You Learn First?

For active traders and short-term players, **technical analysis** is often more immediately useful. Price is the ultimate truth—it's what determines your profit or loss.

For long-term investors, **fundamental analysis** provides the conviction to hold through volatility.

For most people, learning both—even at a basic level—is the optimal path.
