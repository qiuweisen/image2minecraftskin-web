**Quick answer:** compare a futures broker on more than its advertised commission. The useful checklist is **broker commission + exchange/regulatory/clearing or routing fees + market-data and platform costs + margin policy + funding rules + platform connectivity + regulatory status**. A broker advertising a low per-contract rate can still be the wrong fit if its data, routing, margin, or platform model does not match the contract and workflow you trade.

## 1. Compare the full futures cost stack, not the headline commission

A futures order can involve several separate charges. The exact labels vary by broker and routing setup, but the comparison should account for:

1. **Broker commission** — the broker's own per-contract execution charge.
2. **Exchange fee** — charged by the venue where the contract trades.
3. **NFA assessment fee** — a regulatory assessment invoiced through the FCM for applicable futures transactions.
4. **Clearing or routing fee** — may be explicit, bundled, or dependent on the broker/data-routing relationship.
5. **Market-data fee** — can differ by exchange and by professional versus non-professional subscriber status.
6. **Platform or pricing-plan fee** — monthly or one-time charges can change the economics of a lower per-contract rate.
7. **Account-service costs** — wire, withdrawal, inactivity, broker-assisted order, liquidation, or other service fees may apply.

As of July 1, 2026, the NFA states that its futures assessment fee is **$0.01 per side**. NFA currently says the rate will increase to $0.02 per side on July 1, 2027. That is one reason a static “all-in” number copied from an old comparison can become wrong even if the broker's own commission has not changed.

A useful worksheet is:

`Estimated round-trip cost = 2 × per-side transaction charges + allocated data/platform/account costs`

Then run that worksheet for the **actual contract and monthly volume** you expect to trade. MES, ES, MGC, MCL, Bitcoin futures, and foreign-exchange futures do not necessarily carry the same exchange or broker pricing.

## 4. AMP Futures: low published margins and a modular platform/feed model

AMP's current public material emphasizes a different structure: the broker supports **50+ trading platforms** and multiple data feeds rather than pushing every customer into one proprietary front end.

Current official AMP information states:

- **$100 minimum deposit** to activate a live account.
- **$40 MES day-trading margin** in its current margin table.
- Day margin is set by AMP, while maintenance margin is tied to exchange requirements.
- Day margin is **not** described as a liquidation threshold or maximum possible loss.
- AMP supports multiple data feeds and many third-party platforms.

AMP does not make one simple broker-commission number sufficient for every setup. The practical comparison is to request or calculate the **all-in rate for your exact contract, data feed, and platform**.

That is particularly important if switching feeds. AMP notes that changing to another feed can create another set of market-data charges because the exchanges may see them as separate data subscriptions.

| 1,001–10,000 | $0.20 |

For the broader U.S. futures/futures-options schedule, the published starting tier is $0.85 per contract at ≤1,000 monthly contracts, with lower tiers at higher volume. Exchange and regulatory fees are separate as described in IBKR's schedule.

IBKR does **not** publish the same “$40/$50 MES day margin” model shown by some futures-focused brokers. Its futures margin documentation describes **risk-based requirements that can change frequently**, and requirements can depend on residence, exchange, product, account structure, and risk rules.

That difference matters. A trader should not compare NinjaTrader's or AMP's promotional intraday margin directly with an IBKR initial/maintenance figure and call one broker “more expensive.” They represent different risk policies and can change under different conditions.

## Day margin is not an account-size recommendation

A recurring mistake in futures-broker comparisons is treating a published day margin as if it were the amount needed to trade safely.

If a broker lists $40 or $50 for MES, that figure answers a narrow question: **what collateral does this broker currently require to establish the position under its day-margin policy?** It does not answer:

- how much the contract can move against you,
- when the broker can change its requirement,
- what happens around the session close or a volatility event,
- what the maintenance/initial requirement is,
- how liquidation rules operate,
- how much capital fits your risk limit.

AMP explicitly distinguishes day margin from the liquidation trigger and maximum possible loss. NinjaTrader says intraday margins can be adjusted in response to market volatility. IBKR describes futures margin as risk-based and changeable.

For contract value, tick size, and expiration mechanics, use [Futures Contract Expiration, Margin, and Settlement](/blog/futures-contract-expiration-margin-settlement). For position sizing, keep the margin number separate from the amount you are prepared to lose.

## Minimum deposit is not the same as minimum viable capital

Broker pages may state no funding minimum, a $100 account activation minimum, or a contract-specific minimum. Those are **account-opening or access rules**, not a statement that the amount is sufficient for the risk of live futures trading.

Before funding, separate four numbers:

1. **Account-opening minimum** — what is needed to activate the account.
2. **Day margin** — collateral required by the broker for the position during the relevant period.
3. **Initial/maintenance margin** — the higher risk requirement that can apply outside the broker's day-margin window or under exchange/risk rules.
4. **Your own loss budget** — the capital you can lose without violating your risk plan.

Do not substitute one number for another.

## How to compare two futures brokers without being misled by marketing

Use the same contract and the same activity assumptions for both brokers.

### Step 1: Choose one exact contract

Do not compare “Micro commission” at one broker with “standard futures commission” at another. Use the same symbol, such as MES.

### Step 2: Record the broker plan

Free, monthly, lifetime, negotiated, and high-volume schedules can produce different broker commissions.

### Step 3: Add pass-through fees

Record exchange, regulatory, clearing/routing, and any other per-side transaction charges shown by the broker.

### Step 4: Add recurring costs

Market data, platform plans, routing/data-feed subscriptions, and other account fees can matter more than a few cents of commission for a low-volume trader.

### Step 5: Record both day and carry margins

Ask what happens near the daily close, during volatile events, and when the broker changes temporary risk requirements.

### Step 6: Verify the FCM and account protections

For U.S. futures accounts, verify the relevant futures commission merchant and regulatory record rather than relying on a logo or review page. The CFTC requires futures customer funds at FCMs to be segregated from the FCM's own funds for designated-contract-market trading.

Segregation is an important protection, but it should **not** be described as an absolute guarantee against every insolvency loss. The CFTC notes that customer funds in segregation receive a bankruptcy preference; if available customer property is insufficient, remaining claims can still be subject to the insolvency process.

Use the CFTC and NFA's official resources to verify the entity that actually carries or clears the account.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which futures broker has the lowest fees?

There is no single answer without specifying the contract, broker plan, monthly volume, exchange/regulatory/clearing charges, data subscription, and platform costs. Compare the all-in cost for the same contract and activity level rather than the advertised broker commission alone.

### Are NinjaTrader and Tradovate commissions the same?

As of August 9, 2026, their public pricing pages show the same headline Free, Monthly, and Lifetime commission tiers for Micro and Standard futures. Platform workflow and account experience can still differ, and pass-through fees apply.

### Is a $40 or $50 MES margin enough to trade futures safely?

No. A published day margin is a broker collateral requirement, not a recommended account size, stop loss, or maximum possible loss. Margin can change and a futures position can lose more than the amount posted as day margin.

### What is the NFA fee on futures trades in 2026?

NFA states that its assessment fee is $0.01 per side as of July 1, 2026. NFA currently says the fee is scheduled to increase to $0.02 per side on July 1, 2027.

### Does a minimum deposit tell me how much capital I need?

No. The account-opening minimum, day margin, initial/maintenance margin, and your own loss budget are different numbers. A broker can allow a small opening deposit while the contract's actual risk remains much larger.

### Are customer funds protected if a futures broker fails?

U.S. FCMs are subject to CFTC customer-fund segregation requirements for applicable futures accounts. Segregation is a significant safeguard, but it is not the same as a guarantee that every customer will always recover every dollar in every insolvency scenario. Verify the carrying/clearing FCM and read the official risk disclosures.
