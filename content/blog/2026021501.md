## Understanding Scalping: The Foundation

Before implementing any scalping strategy, understanding what makes scalping unique clarifies whether this trading style matches your personality, circumstances, and goals.

### What Is Scalping?

**Definition:** Scalping captures small price movements (typically 5-20 pips in forex, 10-30 cents in stocks, or 0.1-0.5% in crypto) by entering and exiting positions within seconds to minutes, rarely holding positions beyond a few minutes and never overnight.

**Core characteristics:**
- Trade duration: Seconds to minutes (maximum 5-10 minutes)
- Profit targets: Small but frequent (5-20 pip gains typical)
- Stop losses: Tight (5-15 pips typical to maintain reward-risk)
- Trade frequency: 10-50+ trades per day common
- Focus: Intraday noise and micro-trends, not swing moves
- Market exposure: Minimal (no overnight positions)

**What scalpers target:**
- Small price fluctuations that occur constantly
- Momentum bursts from order flow imbalances
- Temporary inefficiencies in bid-ask spreads
- Reactions to news and economic releases
- Breakout movements from consolidation ranges

**What scalpers avoid:**
- Holding through major economic announcements
- Overnight positions and gap risk
- Large price swings (which require wider stops)
- Swing trades lasting hours or days
- Positions that go against the intended direction

### The Mathematics of Scalping

**Why scalping works mathematically:**

**Example profit calculation:**
```
Daily scalping goal: 30 pips net profit

Approach 1: Three 10-pip winners
Trade 1: +10 pips
Trade 2: +10 pips
Trade 3: +10 pips
Total: +30 pips

Approach 2: Five trades, 60% win rate
Trade 1: +12 pips (winner)
Trade 2: +10 pips (winner)
Trade 3: -6 pips (loser)
Trade 4: +14 pips (winner)
Trade 5: -8 pips (loser)
Total: +22 pips

Approach 3: Ten trades, 50% win rate, 2:1 average
5 winners × 12 pips = +60 pips
5 losers × -6 pips = -30 pips
Net: +30 pips
```

**The scalping equation:**
```
Daily Profit = (Win Rate × Average Win) - (Loss Rate × Average Loss)

Example:
Win Rate: 55%
Average Win: 12 pips
Average Loss: 8 pips
Trades per day: 20

Daily Profit = (0.55 × 20 × 12) - (0.45 × 20 × 8)
Daily Profit = 132 - 72 = 60 pips
```

**Monthly compounding effect:**
```
Conservative: 30 pips/day × 20 trading days = 600 pips/month
Moderate: 50 pips/day × 20 trading days = 1,000 pips/month
Aggressive: 100 pips/day × 20 trading days = 2,000 pips/month

On standard lot ($10/pip):
Conservative: $6,000/month
Moderate: $10,000/month
Aggressive: $20,000/month
```

## Scalping Strategies

Successful scalping requires systematic approaches with clear rules for entry, exit, and stop loss placement.

### Strategy 1: Level-Based Scalping

**Concept:** Buy at support, sell at resistance on small timeframes

**Setup requirements:**
```
Timeframe: 1-minute or 5-minute chart
Indicators: None required (price action only)
Trading session: High volatility periods preferred

Requirements:
1. Clear support or resistance level visible
2. Price approaches level with momentum
3. No major news within 30 minutes
4. Spread acceptable (under 1 pip for forex, under 2 cents for stocks)
```

**Entry rules:**
```
Long entry (at support):
- Price touches support level
- Buyer rejection candle forms (hammer, bullish engulfing)
- Enter on close of rejection candle
- OR enter with limit order at support level

Short entry (at resistance):
- Price touches resistance level
- Seller rejection candle forms (shooting star, bearish engulfing)
- Enter on close of rejection candle
- OR enter with limit order at resistance level
```

**Stop loss placement:**
```
Forex: 5-8 pips beyond support/resistance
Stocks: 10-15 cents beyond level
Crypto: 0.15-0.25% beyond level

Example:
Support level: 1.0850
Entry: 1.0852
Stop: 1.0842 (10 pips)
Target: 1.0862 (10 pips)
Reward-risk: 1:1
```

**Profit targets:**
```
Conservative: Next minor support/resistance (5-10 pips)
Moderate: Midpoint of range (10-15 pips)
Aggressive: Next major level (15-20 pips)

Partial profit taking recommended:
- Close 50% at first target (5-8 pips)
- Move stop to breakeven
- Trail remaining position
```

**Example level-based scalp trade:**
```
Asset: EUR/USD
Timeframe: 5-minute

Setup:
Support level identified at 1.0850-1.0853
Price approaches support from above
Bullish rejection candle forms at 1.0851

Entry: 1.0853
Stop: 1.0843 (10 pips)
Target 1: 1.0863 (10 pips, close 50%)
Target 2: 1.0868 (15 pips, close remaining)

Outcome: Target 1 reached in 3 minutes
Remaining closed at Target 2 in 8 minutes
Total profit: 12.5 pips
```

### Strategy 2: Moving Average Momentum Scalping

**Concept:** Trade pullbacks to moving averages in trending markets

**Setup requirements:**
```
Timeframe: 5-minute or 15-minute chart
Indicators:
- 20 EMA (short-term trend)
- 50 EMA (medium-term trend)

Requirements:
1. 20 EMA above 50 EMA (uptrend) or below (downtrend)
2. Price pulls back to 20 EMA
3. Price respects 20 EMA (bounces)
4. No major news pending
```

**Entry rules:**
```
Long entry:
- 20 EMA above 50 EMA (uptrend)
- Price pulls back to touch 20 EMA
- Bullish candle forms at 20 EMA
- Enter on close of candle

Short entry:
- 20 EMA below 50 EMA (downtrend)
- Price pulls back to touch 20 EMA
- Bearish candle forms at 20 EMA
- Enter on close of candle
```

**Stop loss placement:**
```
Long: Below 50 EMA or below recent swing low
Short: Above 50 EMA or above recent swing high

Buffer: 3-5 pips (forex), 10-15 cents (stocks)
```

**Profit targets:**
```
Primary: Recent swing high/low
Secondary: 2:1 reward-risk from entry

Example:
Entry: 1.0850
Stop: 1.0835 (15 pips)
Target: 1.0880 (30 pips, 2:1)
```

**Example MA momentum scalp:**
```
Asset: GBP/USD
Timeframe: 5-minute

Setup:
20 EMA at 1.2650, 50 EMA at 1.2640 (uptrend)
Price pulls back to 1.2652 (touches 20 EMA)
Bullish engulfing forms at 1.2650

Entry: 1.2652
Stop: 1.2635 (below 50 EMA + buffer)
Target: 1.2682 (prior swing high)

Outcome: Target reached in 12 minutes
Profit: 30 pips
```

### Strategy 3: Breakout Scalping

**Concept:** Trade breakouts from consolidation ranges

**Setup requirements:**
```
Timeframe: 1-minute or 5-minute chart
Indicators: Optional (20 EMA for trend filter)

Requirements:
1. Clear consolidation range (5-15 pips wide)
2. Range holds for minimum 10 candles
3. Volume expansion on breakout
4. No major news within 15 minutes
```

**Entry rules:**
```
Conservative entry:
- Wait for pullback to broken level
- Enter on rejection from broken level
- Higher success rate, lower reward-risk

Aggressive entry:
- Enter on breakout candle close
- Higher reward-risk, lower success rate

Best approach:
- Enter 50% on breakout
- Enter 50% on pullback (if it occurs)
```

**Stop loss placement:**
```
Inside consolidation (aggressive):
- Below breakout candle low (longs)
- Above breakout candle high (shorts)

Beyond consolidation (conservative):
- Below opposite side of range
- Lower success rate, safer stops

Buffer: 2-3 pips beyond chosen level
```

**Profit targets:**
```
Measured move:
- Range height added to breakout point
- Example: 10-pip range breakout = 10-pip target

Time-based exit:
- Close position if target not reached in 10-15 minutes
- Scalps should move quickly; stalled moves fail
```

**Example breakout scalp:**
```
Asset: Bitcoin (BTC/USDT)
Timeframe: 5-minute

Setup:
Consolidation range: $42,350-42,450 (15 trading range)
Range holds for 25 candles
Breakout candle closes at $42,470 with volume

Entry: $42,470 (aggressive)
Stop: $42,330 (below breakout candle low)
Target: $42,620 (measured move from breakout)

Outcome: Target reached in 8 minutes
Profit: $150 (0.35%)
```

### Strategy 4: News Scalping

**Concept:** Trade volatility spikes from economic releases

**Setup requirements:**
```
Events to trade:
- Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)
- FOMC interest rate decisions
- CPI inflation reports
- ECB/BOE rate decisions

Preparation:
1. Know consensus forecast
2. Determine likely market reaction
3. Set up straddle orders before release
4. Have exit plan ready
```

**News straddle strategy:**
```
Before news (5 minutes prior):
- Identify support/resistance levels
- Place buy stop 10 pips above current price
- Place sell stop 10 pips below current price
- Set stops at 15 pips from entry
- Set targets at 20-30 pips

After news:
- One order triggered (volatility expands)
- Cancel unfilled order
- Manage active trade with trailing stop
```

**Example news scalp:**
```
Event: NFP release
Pair: EUR/USD
Pre-news price: 1.0850

Orders placed:
Buy stop at 1.0860
Sell stop at 1.0840

News released: Better than expected
Price spikes up: 1.0865 triggers buy stop

Entry: 1.0860
Stop: 1.0845 (15 pips)
Target: 1.0880 (20 pips)

Outcome: Target reached in 90 seconds
Profit: 20 pips
```

**News scalping risks:**
```
Slippage: Execution far from intended price
Spreads widen: Can double from normal
Whipsaws: Price moves both directions rapidly
Reversals: Initial move often reverses

Best practice:
- Reduce position size 50% for news trades
- Use wider stops to accommodate volatility
- Take profits quickly, don't be greedy
- Skip low-probability news events
```

## Risk Management for Scalping

Scalping requires specialized risk management due to high trade frequency and tight margins.

### Position Sizing for Scalpers

**Standard risk per trade:** 0.25-0.5% of account (smaller than swing trading due to frequency)

**Calculation examples:**
```
Account: $10,000
Risk per trade: 0.5% = $50 maximum

Forex example (EUR/USD):
Stop: 10 pips
Pip value: $1 (mini lot) / $10 (standard lot)

Position size calculation:
$50 risk / 10 pips = $5 per pip
Position size: 0.5 mini lots or 5 micro lots

Stock example:
Stop: 15 cents
Risk: $50

Shares calculation:
$50 / $0.15 = 333 shares
Round down: 300 shares
Actual risk: 300 × $0.15 = $45 (0.45%)
```

**Scalping position size tiers:**
```
Conservative scalper:
- 0.25% risk per trade
- 2-3 trades maximum simultaneously
- Maximum daily risk: 0.75%

Moderate scalper:
- 0.5% risk per trade
- 3-5 trades maximum simultaneously
- Maximum daily risk: 1.5%

Aggressive scalper:
- 0.75% risk per trade
- 5-7 trades maximum simultaneously
- Maximum daily risk: 2.25%
```

### Scalping Stop Loss Strategies

**Method 1: Fixed pip stop**
```
Forex: 5-10 pip stop
Stocks: 10-20 cent stop
Crypto: 0.1-0.25% stop

Advantages:
- Simple to calculate
- Position sizing consistent
- Easy to track performance

Disadvantages:
- May be too tight for volatility
- Can get stopped by noise
- Doesn't adapt to market conditions
```

**Method 2: ATR-based stop**
```
Stop distance: 1 × ATR(14)

Example:
EUR/USD 5-minute ATR(14) = 8 pips
Stop distance: 8 pips
Entry: 1.0850
Stop: 1.0842

Advantages:
- Adapts to volatility
- Wider stops in volatile conditions
- Tighter stops in quiet markets

Disadvantages:
- More complex calculation
- Requires ATR indicator
```

**Method 3: Structure-based stop**
```
Stop placement beyond:
- Recent swing high/low
- Support/resistance levels
- Consolidation boundaries

Advantages:
- Respects market structure
- Clear invalidation point
- Not arbitrary

Disadvantages:
- Variable distance between trades
- Requires manual identification
```

### Daily Loss Limits

**Essential protection for scalpers:**

**Recommended daily loss limits:**
```
Conservative: 1.5% of account
Moderate: 2% of account
Maximum: 3% of account

Example:
Account: $10,000
Conservative daily limit: $150 loss
Moderate daily limit: $200 loss
Maximum daily limit: $300 loss

Rule: Stop trading when daily loss limit reached
No exceptions, no "revenge trading"
```

**Daily drawdown tiers:**
```
Tier 1 (-0.5%): Reduce position size 25%
Tier 2 (-1%): Reduce position size 50%
Tier 3 (-1.5%): Stop trading for day

Reset: Tomorrow is new day with full size
```

### Profit Taking for Scalpers

**Strategy 1: Single target exit**
```
Set profit target at entry
Close entire position when target hit
Simple, reduces decision-making

Best for: Beginners, systematic traders
```

**Strategy 2: Partial profit taking**
```
Close 50% at first target (5-8 pips)
Move stop to breakeven
Close remaining 50% at extended target (12-15 pips)
OR trail stop with remaining position

Best for: Intermediate traders, trend continuation
```

**Strategy 3: Trailing stop**
```
Initial target: 2:1 reward-risk
After target reached, trail stop:

Trail method 1 (fixed trail):
Trail stop 5 pips below current price (longs)

Trail method 2 (swing trail):
Move stop below each new swing low

Best for: Capturing extensions, experienced traders
```

## Common Scalping Mistakes to Avoid

Understanding and avoiding common pitfalls accelerates progress and preserves capital.

### Mistake 1: Overtrading

**The problem:** Taking too many trades without proper setup

**Why it happens:**
- Boredom between valid setups
- Revenge trading after losses
- FOMO (missing moves)
- No defined trading plan

**Consequences:**
- Lower quality setups
- Increased transaction costs
- Emotional exhaustion
- Reduced win rate

**Solutions:**
```
Maximum trades per hour: 3-5
Quality checklist: Score setups 1-10, minimum 7 to trade
Mandatory waiting period: 2-3 minutes between trades
Focus on best setups only, not every setup
```

### Mistake 2: Poor Stop Loss Placement

**The problem:** Stops too tight (stopped by noise) or too wide (poor reward-risk)

**Tight stops:**
```
Problem: 3-pip stop on 5-minute chart (too tight)
Result: Stopped out by normal volatility
Solution: Use ATR-based stops (1 × ATR minimum)
```

**Wide stops:**
```
Problem: 20-pip stop for 10-pip target (poor R:R)
Result: Requires 67% win rate to break even
Solution: Reduce target or widen target to achieve 2:1
```

**Optimal stop placement:**
```
Forex: 5-10 pips (depending on pair volatility)
Stocks: 10-20 cents (depending on stock volatility)
Crypto: 0.15-0.3% (depending on coin volatility)

Always calculate reward-risk before entry:
Minimum 1.5:1, preferred 2:1 or better
```

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Spread and Commission

**The problem:** Not accounting for transaction costs in targets

**Example calculation:**
```
Forex trade:
Entry: 1.0850
Target: 1.0860 (10 pips)
Stop: 1.0843 (7 pips)

Apparent reward-risk: 10/7 = 1.43:1

With 1-pip spread and 0.5-pip commission:
Real entry: 1.0850.5
Real target: 1.0859.5 (9.5 pips)
Real stop: 1.0843.5 (7 pips)

Actual reward-risk: 9.5/7 = 1.36:1

Solution: Add spread + commission to stop distance
```

**Stock scalping costs:**
```
Per-share commission: $0.003 per share
500 shares = $1.50 per side = $3.00 round-trip

Profit target: 20 cents
Gross profit: 500 × $0.20 = $100
Net profit: $100 - $3 = $97

Percentage impact: 3%
```

### Mistake 4: Emotional Trading

**The problem:** Letting emotions drive decisions rather than system

**Emotional triggers:**
``
After loss: Revenge trading (immediate re-entry)
After win: Overconfidence (increasing size)
During losing streak: Abandoning system
During winning streak: Breaking rules
```

**Solutions:**
```
1. Written trading plan (follow rules exactly)
2. Predefined daily loss limit (stop when hit)
3. Mandatory break after 2 consecutive losses
4. Trade journal (review emotional decisions)
5. Position size limits (never exceed)
```

### Mistake 5: Wrong Market Conditions

**The problem:** Scalping in conditions unsuitable for strategy

**Poor scalping conditions:**
```
1. Low volatility periods
- ATR at 20% of range or lower
- Price stuck in tight range
- Better: Wait for volatility expansion

2. Major news announcements
- Unpredictable moves

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is scalping suitable for beginners?**

Scalping presents significant challenges for beginners due to rapid decision-making requirements and emotional demands. Most new traders struggle with the fast pace and frequent outcomes inherent to scalping. Beginners typically benefit more from starting with swing trading or longer-term day trading to develop foundational skills, market understanding, and emotional control before attempting scalping. That said, determined beginners can succeed with scalping by starting small, focusing on one simple strategy, and accepting that the learning curve involves months of practice and likely initial losses.

**How much money do I need to start scalping?**

Minimum capital varies by market: $5,000 for forex scalping (with micro lots), $25,000 for stock scalping (US pattern day trader requirement), $2,000-5,000 for crypto scalping, and $5,000-10,000 for futures scalping. These minimums assume conservative risk management (0.25-0.5% per trade). Starting with more capital provides buffer for drawdowns and allows smaller percentage risk while maintaining meaningful absolute dollar gains.

**What is the best timeframe for scalping?**

One-minute and 5-minute charts serve as primary timeframes for most scalpers. One-minute charts provide more signals but more noise; 5-minute charts offer cleaner setups with fewer opportunities. Some scalpers use tick charts (renko, range bars) to filter time-based noise. The best timeframe depends on market volatility, personal preference, and specific strategy—beginners often start with 5-minute charts for cleaner signals.

**Can I scalp with a full-time job?**

Scalping typically requires active market presence during high-volatility periods, making it difficult with full-time employment unless you trade during your free time in active market sessions. Part-time scalpers can trade specific windows (like 8-10 AM EST for London/NY overlap) rather than entire sessions. However, consistent scalping generally requires availability during prime trading hours—swings trading or position trading better suits those with limited market access.

**What win rate do successful scalpers achieve?**

Professional scalpers typically win 50-60% of trades with 1.5:1 to 2:1 average reward-risk ratios. Win rate alone doesn't determine profitability—reward-risk matters equally. A scalper winning 45% with 2:1 average is more profitable than one winning 55% with 1:1 average. Focus on maintaining consistent reward-risk rather than maximizing win rate through tight stops that reduce overall profitability.

**How many trades should I take per day?**

Quality over quantity: 5-15 high-quality trades per day provides sufficient opportunity while maintaining selectivity. Some days offer 20+ valid setups; other days only 2-3. Forcing trades when setups aren't present destroys profitability. Many successful scalpers set daily trade maximums (15-20 trades) to prevent overtrading during losing or winning periods.

**Should I use indicators for scalping or price action only?**

Both approaches work when applied systematically. Price action trading (support/resistance, candlestick patterns) offers fast recognition without indicator lag but requires screen time to develop pattern recognition skills. Indicator-based systems (moving averages, oscillators) provide clear signals but introduce lag. Many scalpers combine both: price action for entry timing, indicators for trend context and confirmation. The best approach is whichever you can execute consistently.

**Why do most scalpers fail?**

Most scalping failures stem from poor risk management (risking too much per trade, overtrading), emotional trading (revenge trading, abandoning systems), inadequate preparation (no written plan, no backtesting), and unrealistic expectations (expecting quick riches without skill development). Successful scalpers treat scalping as a business requiring systematic processes, emotional discipline, and months of practice before achieving consistency.
