**Choose a forex broker by verifying the exact account entity first, then checking its permissions, product, total costs, execution model, withdrawal terms, platform fit, and complaint route.** Do not start with a “best broker” ranking, a minimum-deposit advertisement, or a homepage license badge. A global broker group can place customers from different countries with different legal entities, and those entities may provide different products and protections.

A useful broker comparison therefore begins with this question:

> **Which legal entity will contract with me, and is that entity authorised to offer this specific product to a client in my country?**

This page owns the **beginner broker-selection process**. For the difference between personal trading and regulated brokerage, account management, signals, or advice, use the [Forex license requirements guide](/blog/forex-license-requirements-regulation-guide). For calculating an account balance from minimum trade size, stop distance, costs, and margin, use the [Forex starting-capital calculation](/blog/how-much-money-to-start-forex-trading).

> **Risk note:** Forex and CFD products can produce rapid losses because of leverage. Regulation and due diligence can reduce some provider and fraud risks, but they cannot remove market risk or prove that the product is appropriate for you.

## Key Takeaways

- Verify the **exact account-holding entity**, not only the group brand.
- Confirm the entity's **current status, permissions, contact details, and disciplinary history** in an official register.
- Identify the actual product: rolling spot forex, CFD, futures, options, deliverable currency conversion, or another instrument.
- Compare **total cost**, not only the advertised minimum spread.
- Use a demo to test workflow, not as proof of live execution or withdrawal quality.
- Read the client agreement, order-execution policy, fee schedule, margin rules, and complaint process before funding.
- Treat rankings, awards, affiliate reviews, social-media claims, and a successful small withdrawal as supporting evidence—not substitutes for official verification.

## The Seven-Step Forex Broker Selection Process

| Step | Question | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Account entity | Who will legally hold the account? | Client agreement, application disclosure, legal name, company number, registered address |
| 2. Authorisation | Can that entity offer this product to you? | Official register status, permissions, restrictions, branch or cross-border status |
| 3. Product fit | What contract are you actually trading? | Product disclosure, contract specification, counterparty and ownership structure |
| 4. Total cost | What can the full trade lifecycle cost? | Spread data, commission, financing, conversion and non-trading fee schedules |
| 5. Execution | How are orders priced, filled, rejected, and closed? | Execution policy, order types, margin close-out, slippage and conflict disclosures |
| 6. Money movement | How are deposits and withdrawals handled? | Accepted methods, account-name rules, processing stages, fees and complaint path |
| 7. Operational fit | Can you use and review the service reliably? | Platform/device test, statements, export, support response and account closure process |

A broker that fails Step 1 or Step 2 should not advance because a low spread or attractive platform cannot repair an unverifiable account relationship.

## Step 1: Identify the Exact Legal Entity

A trading brand is not always the company that signs the account agreement. The same group may operate through separate entities in the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, Australia, and other jurisdictions.

Before comparing features, record:

- the legal name shown in the account application;
- the company or registration number;
- the regulator and reference number claimed for that entity;
- the registered office;
- the country whose law governs the agreement;
- the entity named as counterparty or service provider;
- the complaint and dispute-resolution body;
- whether another company handles payments, custody, support, or introducing activity.

Do not assume the strongest license displayed in a website footer applies to you. Check the final application page and client agreement. If the legal entity changes during registration, repeat the verification for the new entity.

### Watch for clone-firm mismatches

A fraudulent site may copy the legal name or registration number of a real firm while using a different domain, email address, phone number, payment account, or social-media profile.

Match the official-register record against:

- website domain;
- email domain;
- telephone number;
- physical address;
- approved trading names;
- principal firm, branch, or appointed-representative relationship.

The FCA explains that its Firm Checker and Financial Services Register show permissions, trading names, contact details, client-money information, historic fines, and appointed-representative relationships. It also warns that an appointed representative may only perform activities allowed by its principal.

## Step 2: Verify Status, Permissions, and History

The word “regulated” is incomplete. The relevant questions are:

1. Is the exact entity listed?
2. Is its status active?
3. Does it have permission for the product and service offered?
4. Is it permitted to serve customers in your location?
5. Are there restrictions, warnings, disciplinary actions, or past names?
6. Do the contact details match independently?

### United States

The CFTC advises prospective OTC Forex customers to verify the dealer and its employees, including registration and disciplinary history, before depositing money or providing personal information. NFA's BASIC database is the public due-diligence tool for registration, membership, principals, branches, and regulatory actions.

For a U.S. retail Forex account, confirm the relevant entity and activity rather than relying on a parent company's securities, futures, or overseas registration.

### United Kingdom

Use the FCA Firm Checker for a current consumer check and the Financial Services Register for full records and historical information. Verify the permitted services, trading names, website and contact information, client-money status, restrictions, and complaint information.

### European Union and EEA

ESMA states that firms can only provide investment services if authorised and directs investors to national competent-authority registers and its public register. Confirm that the entity is active and authorised for the relevant investment service. A group license in one country does not automatically prove that every entity can market every product in every member state.

### Australia

Use ASIC's Professional Registers and check current scam and enforcement information. A current license record does not end the review: verify the authorised services, product issuer, target-market documents, and any stop orders or restrictions.

ASIC's 2026 review of CFD issuers reported continuing compliance problems and significant retail losses despite the sector being regulated. That is a useful reminder that authorisation is a minimum filter, not a performance or suitability certificate.

## Step 3: Identify the Product You Will Trade

“Forex account” can refer to different legal and economic products:

- leveraged OTC rolling spot Forex;
- a contract for difference referencing a currency pair;
- exchange-traded currency futures or options;
- deliverable currency conversion;
- spread betting where available;
- a managed or copy-trading arrangement;
- an account introduced by another company.

The distinction affects:

- who is the counterparty;
- how price and execution are determined;
- margin and close-out rules;
- overnight financing;
- tax and reporting treatment;
- complaint and compensation routes;
- whether the customer owns or receives currency;
- what protections and disclosures apply.

Do not choose a provider merely because it offers the pair you want. Confirm the instrument type, contract size, minimum order, price source, trading hours, expiry or rollover mechanics, and account classification.

## Step 4: Compare Total Trading and Account Costs

A headline such as “spreads from 0.0” does not reveal what your trades will cost. The relevant cost depends on the exact account, pair, size, session, order type, holding period, and account currency.

Build a comparison sheet with these fields:

| Cost field | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Spread | Typical or historical spread for the intended pair and session, not only the minimum |
| Commission | Per side or round trip, minimum ticket charge, volume tier and account type |
| Overnight financing | Long and short rate, calculation basis, triple-charge day and holiday treatment |
| Currency conversion | Rate or markup when P&L, fees, or deposits differ from account currency |
| Market data | Any subscription, exchange, professional-status, or data entitlement charge |
| Inactivity | Trigger period, amount, affected account types and reactivation process |
| Deposit and withdrawal | Method, intermediary-bank, card, e-wallet or currency-conversion charges |
| Guaranteed or special orders | Additional premium, wider spread, eligibility or refund condition |
| Account closure | Residual balance, transfer and dormant-account procedures |

For a planned trade, compare:

```text
Estimated lifecycle cost =
entry spread and commission
+ exit spread and commission
+ expected financing
+ conversion charges
+ applicable non-trading costs
```

Do not combine unlike products or accounts. A commission account and a spread-only account need to be compared using the same pair, size, session, and holding period.

The [Forex spread guide](/blog/forex-spread-explained-transaction-costs) covers spread mechanics. The starting-capital page explains why costs, minimum order size, and margin must be tested together rather than treated as separate marketing figures.

## Step 5: Read the Execution and Margin Documents

In OTC Forex, the dealer may be your counterparty and may control the trading platform information you see. The CFTC specifically tells customers to understand that they are trading against the dealer, to compare prices with third-party sources, and to investigate the firm before depositing.

Read the broker's current documents for:

- market and pending-order handling;
- price sources and spread construction;
- slippage and price improvement;
- rejected, requoted, or partially filled orders;
- stop-loss treatment during gaps or fast markets;
- margin calculation and close-out level;
- negative-balance policy and exceptions;
- conflicts of interest and counterparty model;
- system outages and error-trade policy;
- corporate actions or contract rollovers where relevant;
- statement and transaction-confirmation content.

A beginner does not need to prefer one execution label such as “ECN,” “STP,” or “market maker” without context. Those labels can be used differently across firms and accounts. Compare the legally binding execution policy, fee schedule, contract specifications, and actual records.

### What a demo can and cannot test

A demo can help verify:

- whether the platform runs on your device;
- chart and order-ticket navigation;
- supported order types;
- available pairs and contract specifications;
- statement and export layout;
- general support documentation.

A demo does not prove:

- live liquidity or fill quality;
- slippage distribution;
- rejection or partial-fill behavior;
- live spread and financing conditions;
- deposit and withdrawal processing;
- margin interventions;
- complaint handling;
- platform behavior during a real outage or stressed market.

Use the [Forex trading on a Mac guide](/blog/forex-trading-mac-platforms-guide) if device compatibility is a material requirement. For chart practice without opening a broker account, ChartMini can replay historical candles, but it does not test a broker's live execution.

## Step 6: Investigate Funding, Withdrawals, and Account Control

CFTC fraud advisories identify common warning signs including social-media solicitation, requests for cryptocurrency or other hard-to-reverse payments, manipulated account results, unusually high leverage, and ignored or refused withdrawals.

Before funding, verify:

- whether deposits and withdrawals must use an account in your own name;
- which entity receives the money;
- accepted currencies and conversion method;
- processing stages and stated timeframes;
- withdrawal fees and minimums;
- source-of-funds and identity-document requirements;
- how unused funds and open positions are handled during closure;
- where a formal complaint is filed;
- whether an ombudsman, arbitration forum, or regulator can review the dispute.

A small successful withdrawal can test part of the process, but it is not proof that future withdrawals will be successful. Fraud advisories note that scammers may permit an early withdrawal to build confidence. Treat it as one operational data point, not a substitute for regulatory and legal verification.

Never send additional “tax,” “unlock,” “liquidity,” “insurance,” or “recovery” payments solely because a platform says they are required to release a displayed balance. Verify the obligation independently through official documents and the relevant authority.

## Step 7: Test Operational Fit Before Depositing

A regulated firm can still be the wrong operational choice. Use a demo, documentation, and pre-sales support to test the tasks you actually need.

### Platform and account checklist

- Does the platform support your operating system and accessibility needs?
- Are the required pairs, order sizes, and order types available in your entity and account?
- Can you see margin, financing, spread, and commission information before confirming an order?
- Can you export statements and transaction records?
- Are timestamps and account currency clear?
- Is two-factor authentication available?
- Can you restrict or revoke third-party access?
- Are API, Expert Advisor, VPS, copy-trading, or hedging functions permitted for this account?
- What happens if the platform, price feed, or internet connection fails?
- Can support explain the legal entity, product, and fee schedule without giving contradictory answers?

Save the documents and screenshots used for the decision. Broker terms, product availability, fees, and legal entities can change.

## Red Flags That End the Review

Stop the process when you encounter any of these:

- the legal entity cannot be identified;
- the official register does not match the domain or contact details;
- the firm is unauthorised for the offered service;
- the salesperson tells you not to contact the regulator or bank;
- guaranteed returns, fixed win rates, or “risk-free” leveraged trading;
- pressure to deposit immediately or increase funding;
- remote-access requests to control your computer or banking app;
- payment to a personal account or unrelated company;
- crypto-only payment without a clear regulated-account reason;
- unexplained fees required to release withdrawals;
- fabricated celebrity, media, regulator, or award endorsements;
- no accessible client agreement, product disclosure, fee schedule, or complaint policy;
- a demand to misstate residence, experience, income, professional status, or product knowledge.

Do not let a low minimum deposit, high leverage, bonus, account manager, signal service, or social-media group override these failures.

## How to Use Rankings and Reviews Without Being Misled

Rankings can help create a shortlist, but they reflect a methodology, location, testing date, commercial model, and selected account type.

Before using a ranking, check:

- which country and customer type it covers;
- whether the publisher receives affiliate compensation;
- when spreads, fees, and products were tested;
- whether the reviewer opened the same legal-entity account available to you;
- whether the score combines regulation, platform, education, cost, and product range in a way that matches your priorities;
- whether negative findings and methodology are visible.

User reviews are most useful for identifying repeated claims to investigate, such as withdrawals, outages, support delays, verification problems, or unexpected charges. They do not independently prove those claims, and a high star rating does not replace official records or contractual terms.

## Common Questions

### What should a beginner check first when choosing a forex broker?

First identify the exact legal entity that would hold the account, then verify that entity and its permitted activities in the official register for the jurisdiction serving you. A group brand or license shown elsewhere on the website does not prove that the same entity will hold your account.

### Does regulation guarantee that a forex broker is safe?

No. Authorisation can provide supervision, conduct rules, complaint routes, and other protections, but it does not prevent market losses, guarantee every withdrawal, or make a leveraged product suitable. You still need to check the entity, permissions, history, costs, terms, and product risks.

### Should I choose the forex broker with the lowest spread?

Not from a headline spread alone. Compare the total cost for the account, product, order size, session, and holding period you expect to use, including spread, commission, financing, conversion, inactivity, data, and withdrawal charges where applicable.

### Can a demo account prove that live forex execution is good?

No. A demo can test platform navigation, available order controls, charting, and general workflow. It does not prove live liquidity, rejection rates, slippage, partial fills, financing, withdrawal handling, or how the broker applies live account rules.

### Are online forex broker reviews enough for due diligence?

No. Reviews can reveal recurring themes to investigate, but identities, incentives, dates, account entities, and circumstances may be unclear. Use reviews only after checking official registers, legal documents, fee schedules, warnings, enforcement history, and complaint routes.

### How much should I deposit with a new forex broker?

There is no universal deposit amount. First verify the broker and calculate whether its minimum trade size, margin, stop distance, costs, and account currency fit money you can afford to lose. A broker minimum deposit is an access condition, not a recommendation.
