## Start a No-Signup Replay Session

### 1. Open the simulator

Go to [ChartMini's simulator](/play). You should reach the practice screen without an account-creation step.

### 2. Choose a market category

Select one of the available categories:

- **Stocks** for daily equity-chart practice;
- **Forex** for currency-pair price-action practice;
- **Crypto** for cryptocurrency chart practice.

The category is your choice, but the normal daily replay session is random. Do not expect to choose a specific symbol such as AAPL, EUR/USD, or BTC/USD from this page.

### 3. Choose a replay mode

ChartMini currently provides two daily replay approaches:

- **Random segment:** practice a randomly selected section of historical data;
- **Random time:** begin from a random point in the available historical record.

Both modes are intended to reduce hindsight. You make decisions while future candles remain unavailable.

### 4. Advance the chart one candle at a time

Use the next-candle control instead of scrolling through a completed chart. Before advancing, decide what the current chart information supports:

- buy;
- sell or short;
- hold an existing simulated position;
- take no action.

“No trade” is a valid practice decision. The purpose is to review how you interpreted the chart, not to maximize the number of clicks.

### 5. Use a realistic quantity

The simulator uses virtual cash and lets you adjust position quantity. Avoid choosing an oversized position merely because the money is simulated.

ChartMini's daily replay controls are intentionally simple. They let you buy, sell, adjust quantity, and move the chart forward. They are not a full broker order ticket. Write any planned stop, target, or invalidation condition in a separate journal rather than assuming the simulator has placed those orders for you.

### 6. Finish and review the session

At the end of the session, review both the outcome and the decisions that produced it. Useful questions include:

- Did you act before the candle confirmed your condition?
- Did you change the rule after seeing price move?
- Did you increase quantity after a loss?
- Did you confuse a winning outcome with a good decision?
- Did you record why you stayed out of a trade?

A replay session is most useful when it produces a specific next practice goal.

## What This Simulator Is Best For

ChartMini is best suited to a few narrow practice tasks.

### Candle-by-candle chart reading

Historical candles appear progressively, so you cannot use the completed chart as an answer key. This is useful for practicing trend, range, support and resistance, breakout, pullback, and candlestick interpretation.

### Directional decision practice

You can record whether the available chart information supported a long, short, or no-trade decision. The result can then be reviewed without claiming that the same decision would receive an identical live fill.

### Position-quantity discipline

Because the interface includes simulated cash and quantity controls, you can practice avoiding oversized positions and impulsive changes. The exercise is behavioral; it is not a complete position-sizing or broker-margin model.

### Cross-market observation

Stocks, forex, and crypto are available as separate market categories. Use them to observe how chart behavior differs, but do not assume a setup transfers unchanged from one market to another.

## What It Does Not Simulate

A no-signup browser tool is not a replacement for every type of trading practice.

ChartMini's daily replay does not provide:

- live broker order routing;
- real-money trading;
- guaranteed selection of a specific symbol or historical date;
- Level 2, order-book, DOM, or time-and-sales simulation;
- broker-specific market, limit, stop, stop-limit, bracket, or trailing-order behavior;
- exact bid/ask spread, commissions, slippage, latency, partial fills, or exchange queues;
- synchronized cloud history across devices through a user account;
- proof that a strategy is profitable or that a user is ready to trade live.

The CFTC notes that hypothetical or simulated results have inherent limitations because trades are not actually executed and may not reflect liquidity or other market factors. Use replay results as practice evidence, not as a forecast of future returns.

A no-signup simulator is useful for starting quickly, but an account-based platform may be more appropriate when you need:

- progress synchronized across devices;
- a persistent cloud journal;
- an exact symbol or date selector;
- live or delayed quotes;
- broker-specific order-entry practice;
- options chains, futures contract details, margin rules, or other specialized instruments;
- detailed execution reports and account statements.

Account creation is not automatically a disadvantage. It becomes worthwhile when the saved data or platform-specific workflow is part of what you need to practice.

## Common No-Signup Simulator Mistakes

### Treating random replay as a prediction game

The hidden future is meant to test a decision process. Guessing whether the next candle will be green or red without a stated reason creates little reusable evidence.

### Changing the market after every loss

Switching from stocks to forex to crypto after each poor outcome prevents comparable review. Keep the category and practice question stable long enough to identify a repeated behavior.

### Using simulated cash unrealistically

Oversized quantities can make the session feel exciting while training behavior that would be unacceptable with real money. Use quantities that keep the decision review meaningful.

### Assuming a buy or sell click represents a broker fill

ChartMini uses a simplified historical-bar simulation. A live order can be affected by bid/ask pricing, liquidity, order type, latency, partial execution, fees, and broker rules.

### Judging only by profit and loss

A profitable simulated trade may come from a poor decision, and a losing trade may still follow a reasonable predefined rule. Review rule adherence separately from the outcome.

### Expecting browser-local storage to behave like an account

A no-signup session may rely on data stored in the browser. Clearing browser storage, changing browsers, using private mode, or switching devices can remove or separate that data.

## A Simple Review Template

Use a small journal beside the simulator:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Market category | Stocks, forex, or crypto |
| Replay mode | Random segment or random time |
| Chart observation | Trend, range, level, pattern, or uncertainty |
| Decision | Buy, sell, hold, or no trade |
| Invalidation | What would show the idea was wrong |
| Quantity reason | Why the size was reasonable for the simulated account |
| Rule followed? | Yes, no, or unclear |
| Next practice goal | One behavior to repeat or correct |

You do not need a fixed number of trades before reviewing. Review often enough to identify repeated decisions, and avoid claiming statistical confidence from a small or selectively chosen sample.
