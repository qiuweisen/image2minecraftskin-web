## What Is Thinkorswim PaperMoney?

Thinkorswim PaperMoney is the simulated trading environment built into Charles Schwab's [thinkorswim](https://www.schwab.com/trading/thinkorswim) platform suite. It is used to practice trading with virtual money before placing live trades.

It is important to understand that thinkorswim includes desktop, web, and mobile versions, and paperMoney is a core part of the thinkorswim platform environment. You can access the simulated trading features whether you are at your desk or using the mobile app. For more background on how simulation tools work, check out our [paper trading guide](/blog/what-is-paper-trading-ultimate-guide).

## Who Is Thinkorswim PaperMoney Best For?

Thinkorswim PaperMoney is a powerful tool, but it fits certain profiles better than others. 

**It is best for:**
* Schwab users who already hold or plan to open an account.
* Options traders who need detailed options chains and Greeks calculation.
* Traders practicing broker-style workflows and complex order entry.
* Users who want to learn the thinkorswim interface before risking real capital.

**It is not ideal for:**
* Users who only want quick chart replay without complex platform overhead.
* Beginners who do not want to register for a brokerage account yet.
* People who want weekend or off-hours historical chart practice (where dedicated replay tools excel).
* Users seeking a lightweight, no-signup simulator.

## Thinkorswim PaperMoney Pros

* **Realistic broker-style interface:** The paper trading environment closely mirrors the live platform, helping users become familiar with order tickets and platform workflows before trading with real capital.
* **Multi-asset support:** You can practice trading stocks, options, futures, and forex all in one place.
* **Strong charting and studies:** The platform comes with hundreds of built-in technical indicators and the ability to create custom studies.
* **Order ticket practice:** It is highly useful for learning complex order tickets and mastering platform workflows.
* **Available across the thinkorswim platform suite:** You can seamlessly switch between the desktop, web, and mobile versions.

## Thinkorswim PaperMoney Cons

* **Learning curve:** The professional-grade interface can be overwhelming for beginners.
* **Schwab ecosystem requirement:** Full access requires a Schwab account, although Schwab offers a Guest Pass for users who want to try the platform first.
* **Not the fastest tool for chart replay practice:** While it has an OnDemand feature, dedicated [market replay and chart replay](/blog/what-is-market-replay-backtesting) tools are generally faster and easier to set up.
* **Unrealistic habits:** Large virtual balances can sometimes create unrealistic position-sizing habits if not managed carefully.
* **Different from replay training:** Broker-style simulation is not the same as historical replay training, which is often better for rapid pattern recognition.
* **No live guarantees:** Simulated performance does not guarantee live trading performance due to emotional differences and real market liquidity.

## Thinkorswim PaperMoney vs ChartMini

ChartMini is not a full broker simulator like Thinkorswim PaperMoney. It is a better fit when you want fast, browser-based chart replay practice without opening a Schwab account, installing software, or learning a complex trading platform.

Use Thinkorswim PaperMoney if you want to practice Schwab platform mechanics, options orders, or broker-style execution. 
Use ChartMini if you want to practice reading charts, replaying historical price action, and making directional decisions quickly.

## Thinkorswim PaperMoney vs TradingView

TradingView is stronger as a general charting and social platform. Its aesthetic charts and massive community of custom indicators make it a favorite for pure analysis. On the other hand, Thinkorswim is stronger for Schwab-specific platform practice and complex options workflows. 

It is also worth noting that TradingView's Bar Replay and Paper Trading are separate workflows. According to TradingView, trading orders placed while Bar Replay is running are executed based on real-time data, not the replayed historical bars. Certain intraday replay features also require a paid plan. Check TradingView's [official pricing page](https://www.tradingview.com/pricing/) for current tier details.

## Thinkorswim PaperMoney vs NinjaTrader

NinjaTrader is more futures-focused, catering heavily to the futures day trading community with advanced order flow tools. Thinkorswim is broader, offering a wider variety of asset classes but tied specifically to the Schwab ecosystem. NinjaTrader may be stronger for futures replay and simulation workflows, but it has a steeper setup path and is primarily a desktop application.

## FAQ

### Is Thinkorswim PaperMoney free?
Yes, Thinkorswim PaperMoney is free to use, provided you have a Charles Schwab brokerage account.

### Do I need a Schwab account to use Thinkorswim PaperMoney?
A Schwab account is required to log in to the full thinkorswim platform suite. Schwab also offers a Guest Pass for users who want to try the platform before opening an account.

### Is Thinkorswim PaperMoney good for beginners?
It can be overwhelming for absolute beginners due to its professional-grade tools and steep learning curve, but it is excellent for those committed to learning broker-style workflows or options trading.

### Can I use Thinkorswim PaperMoney in a browser?
Yes, thinkorswim offers a web version (thinkorswim web) alongside its primary desktop application and mobile app.

### What is the best Thinkorswim PaperMoney alternative?
The best alternative depends on your goals. ChartMini is best for fast chart replay practice, TradingView is best for charting aesthetics, NinjaTrader is best for futures, and FX Replay is best for forex.

### Is ChartMini a replacement for Thinkorswim PaperMoney?
No, ChartMini is not a full broker simulator. It is a lightweight alternative focused specifically on historical chart replay and price action practice.

### Can paper trading make me profitable?
Paper trading helps build mechanical skills and test strategies without financial risk, but it does not guarantee profitability in live markets, as it cannot fully replicate emotional pressure, real slippage, or live liquidity conditions.

### What is the difference between paper trading and chart replay?
Paper trading typically involves placing simulated orders against a live or delayed market feed in real time. Chart replay involves stepping backward and forward through historical price bars at your own pace to practice reading setups.
